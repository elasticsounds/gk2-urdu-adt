Eid-ul-Adha and Milad-un-Nabi

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 27 of 96 (book page 23).

Eid-ul-Adha

Eid-ul-Adha is also called the Big Eid. This Eid is celebrated in memory of the sacrifice of Hazrat Ibrahim (peace be upon him).

On this day Muslims sacrifice animals. They eat the meat of the sacrifice themselves and also share it with the poor and with their relatives.

Picture: People in new clothes stand in a courtyard in front of a mosque with pink domes; a man and two children are beside a white goat, and there are more goats behind them.

Do you know? Eid-ul-Adha is celebrated on the 10th of Zil-Hajj.

Milad-un-Nabi (peace and blessings be upon him, his family and his companions)

Muslims celebrate Milad-un-Nabi on the 12th of Rabi-ul-Awwal with religious devotion and respect. On this day, gatherings of Durood and Naat are held in honour of Hazrat Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, the Last of the Prophets (peace and blessings be upon him, his family and his companions), and food is shared out among the people.

Picture: A white mosque stands under a blue night sky, its dome and minarets decorated with tiny lights, and colourful bunting hangs above it.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Both festivals have one thing in common: sharing. Meat is shared on Eid-ul-Adha and food on Milad-un-Nabi. To remember the dates: “10 — Big Eid, 12 — Milad”.
  • Vision: In the second picture there are tiny lights on the mosque. Ask a grown-up to tell you about the picture, or make the shape of a dome and a minaret from clay or dough and feel them.
  • Hearing: Naat and Durood are things we listen to. Show the child their words in writing or in signs, and show a picture or video of a gathering where people are reciting together.
  • For teachers: When you talk about the sacrifice, put the stress on sharing and on caring for the poor. Build the habit of reciting Durood whenever the name of the Holy Prophet (peace be upon him) is mentioned, and make it easy for children of other religions to ask questions too.
