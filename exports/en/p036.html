For teachers: Tell the children more about natural and material resources. Explain why it is important to use natural resources carefully.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: For Question 3, think how the thing would feel if you touched it: hard and cold (iron), shiny and yellow (gold), see-through (glass) or brown and grainy (wood). For Question 4, remember the picture of the hand pump on page 30.
  • Vision: Instead of pictures, give real things to hold: a wooden desk, a glass tumbler (with care), a metal key and a ring or bangle. The child can find out the material by touching it, feeling its weight and tapping it gently.
  • Movement: Instead of writing in the blanks, the child can say the answers or point to iron things in the room (a door latch, a nail, a window bar). A partner or the teacher can write them down.
  • For teachers: Link caring for natural resources to everyday jobs: turning off the tap, writing on both sides of the paper, watering a plant. Let every child make one “I will do this” promise, whether by speaking, by signs or with a picture.

Answers

Question 3

  • 1. Table: Wood
  • 2. Tumbler: Glass
  • 3. Ring: Gold and a precious stone
  • 4. Fan: Iron

Question 4

  • Sample answer: (i) hand pump (ii) nail (iii) lock. (Hammer, key, scissors and gate are also right answers.)

Question 5

  • Sample answer: (i) We get food (fruit) from trees. (ii) We get wood from trees. (This is also right: trees give shade and keep the air clean.)
