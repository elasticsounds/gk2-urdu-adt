Chapter 5: Natural environment and resources

Learning outcomes

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

  • Recognise that the natural environment is made up of living and non-living things.
  • Know that natural resources such as land, air, water and the sun are very important for human life.
  • Know the difference between natural things and things made by people.
  • Recognise that people use natural resources to make the things that humans need.

Living and non-living

Dear children! Our Earth is made up of water and land, where living and non-living things are found, for example green fields, mountains, animals and human beings.

Living and non-living things are very important for one another. For example, we get food from plants and we use water for drinking. All living and non-living things together make up the environment.

All living things need air to stay alive, and food and water to eat and drink. Living things grow, thrive and move, but non-living things do not grow, do not move and do not have young ones.

Try it

Which thing is living and which is non-living? Put each thing in the right box.

Groups: Living; Non-living.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: To spot a living thing, ask three questions: Does it grow? Does it need food, water and air? Does it have babies (or seeds)? If the answer is yes, it is living.
  • Vision: Hold real things in your hands and feel them: a leaf or a plant in a pot (living) and a stone or a pencil (non-living). Feel your breath on your hand. You are living too!
  • Understanding: Remember just two things: a living thing breathes and grows; a non-living thing does not grow. For each thing, show a thumbs up for living or a thumbs down for non-living.
  • For teachers: Take the children to the window or out into the courtyard and show them living and non-living things. Bring things (a leaf, a stone, a bottle of water) to children who cannot walk. Accept answers given by pointing as well.
