General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 71 of 96 (book page 67).

Chapter 11: Heat and light

Learning outcomes

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

  • Identify the sources of heat and light (at home, at school and in the surroundings).
  • Sort the sources of heat and light into natural and artificial ones.
  • Describe the ways in which we can produce heat.
  • Make a list of the uses of heat and light.
  • Recognise that heat and light feel stronger when we are near their sources.

Picture: The sun is shining in an orange sky, and its light is falling on the water below.

Picture: A round full moon, many stars and white clouds are in the blue night sky.

The sun is the natural and the biggest source of heat and light. Besides the sun, we also get light from the moon and the stars.

Try it

Answer the questions about the lights in the sky.

  • a) stars
  • b) clouds

3. The sun gives us light and ___.

  • a) water
  • b) soil
  • c) heat

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Remember: heat means warmth, and light is what lets us see. The sun gives us both.
  • Vision: Hold your hand out in the sunshine and feel the sun's heat on your skin, then move it into the shade. This way you can tell where the sun is without seeing it.
  • Hearing: There are two new words: "heat" and "light". For heat, use the sign of warming your hands at a fire. For light, use the sign of opening a closed fist.
  • For teachers: Start the chapter by switching the room light off and on, and by letting children hold a hand in the sunshine at the window, so every child experiences light and heat. Remind them that looking straight at the sun harms the eyes.

Answers

Try it

  • 2. a) stars
