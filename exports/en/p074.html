Near and far; Exercise

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 74 of 96 (book page 70).

Look and tell

Look at the pictures carefully: where is the thing near and where is it far? Then choose the answer.

1. In which picture is more light from the torch reaching the bucket?

  • a) The bucket is far from the girl
  • b) The bucket is near the girl

2. In which picture is more heat from the fire reaching the boy?

  • a) The boy sits near the fireplace
  • b) The boy sits far from the fireplace

3. Why does this happen?

  • a) Because far things are bigger
  • b) Because everything is cold at night
  • c) Because heat and light are stronger near their source

Picture: A girl shines a torch on a bucket placed far away; the light spreads out and becomes dim, and the bucket looks faint.

Picture: The same girl shines the torch on a bucket placed near her; the bucket is bright and clear.

Picture: A boy sits at the other side of the room, far from the burning fireplace.

Picture: A boy sits right next to the burning fireplace, warming his hands.

Exercise

Question 1

Read or listen to each sentence and say: is it true or false?

  • 1. The sun is the biggest source of light. (True / False)
  • 2. Standing near a fireplace or heater gives less heat. (True / False)
  • 3. We can see in the dark. (True / False)
  • 4. Burning wood gives light and heat. (True / False)

Question 2

Put each thing in the right box: a natural source or made by people? (The rest of this question's words are on the next page; here they are all given together.)

Groups: Natural source; Made by people.

Items:

  • Bulb
  • Candle
  • Heater
  • Stars
  • Sun

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: A way to remember: 'near means more, far means less'. Say it again and again while showing near and far with your hands.
  • For everyone: Sit at a safe distance to get warmth from a fire or heater. Going too close is dangerous.
  • Vision: Instead of the pictures, feel it yourself: slowly move your hand towards a sunny window or a closed pot of warm water (without touching) — the heat grows as you come near and fades as you move away.
  • For teachers: Darken the room and move a torch near to the wall and then away from it, so that the children see the difference in light for themselves. For true or false, also accept tick and cross cards, a thumbs up or down, or eye-pointing, and read every sentence aloud.

Answers

Look and tell

  • 2. a) The boy sits near the fireplace
  • 3. c) Because heat and light are stronger near their source
