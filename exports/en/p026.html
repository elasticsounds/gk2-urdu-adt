Eid-ul-Fitr

Muslims celebrate Eid-ul-Fitr on the 1st of Shawwal, after the fasts of the holy month of Ramadan are complete. On this day, after offering the Eid prayer, Muslims meet their relatives and friends and wish them a happy Eid. They visit their homes and give Eidi to the children.

So that poor Muslims can share in this happiness, Sadaqat-ul-Fitr (Fitrana) is given before the Eid prayer.

Picture: On the night before Eid, in a bazaar decorated with colourful bunting, two boys shake hands and say Eid Mubarak, a man opens his arms to welcome a guest, three girls stand in new clothes, and the new moon is in the sky.

Think about it!

What do you like to eat on the day of Eid-ul-Fitr?

  • 1. Close your eyes and remember the festival day.
  • 2. Say the name of your favourite food, or draw it.
  • 3. Ask your partner what they like.

Activity

Make Eid cards for your friends and the school staff.

  • 1. Fold a sheet of paper in half to make a card.
  • 2. On the front, draw a moon, a star, a flower or a mosque and colour it in.
  • 3. Inside, write “Eid Mubarak” and your name.
  • 4. Give the card to your friend, your teacher or someone else at school.

Try it

How is Eid-ul-Fitr celebrated? See how much you remember.

Put these in the right order:

  • Fitrana is given before the Eid prayer.
  • Muslims offer the Eid prayer.
  • The fasts of Ramadan are completed.
  • They wish their relatives and friends a happy Eid and give Eidi to children.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Look at the picture: people are shaking hands and smiling. Eid means being happy together. Remember the order: first the fasts, then Fitrana, then the prayer, then greetings and Eidi.
  • Vision: On the Eid card, draw the moon and star with glue and stick lentils, sand or wool on them, so that they can be felt by touch.
  • Movement: If cutting or writing is hard, use thick crayons, finger paints or stickers. A partner can help to fold the paper and write “Eid Mubarak”.
  • For teachers: In “Think about it!”, let children who do not celebrate Eid talk about the food of their own festival. The card can carry greetings for any festival, not only “Eid Mubarak”. Accept answers that are spoken, signed or drawn.
