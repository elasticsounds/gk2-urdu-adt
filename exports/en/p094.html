The lion's justice

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 94 of 96 (book page 90).

Now the fox asked the elephant to tell his story. The elephant looked towards the lion and said: "Your Majesty! This monkey is lying. I have not gone towards his area for many days. I had gone to visit the neighbouring jungle."

The elephant was sure that, since the lion was his friend, he would decide in his favour. The lion asked the other animals whether anyone had seen the elephant in the monkey's area.

The bear and the pigeon came forward and said: "Yes! The monkey is telling the truth. We saw the elephant troubling him." The lion looked at the elephant and said: "You not only troubled the monkey but also told a lie. Your punishment is to serve the monkey for one month and collect fruit for him."

All the animals were pleased with the lion's justice. The elephant was ashamed of his behaviour and admitted his mistake. The elephant then served the monkey for one month.

This story teaches us that we should always show justice and impartiality.

Activity

Tell the class a story with a moral, and discuss the lesson it teaches.

  • 1. Choose a story you have heard at home or school.
  • 2. Tell the story, show it with pictures or act it out.
  • 3. Say: what is the lesson of this story?
  • 4. Ask your friends what they learned.

Try it

Remember the story of the lion's decision and play the game.

Put these in the right order:

  • The bear and the pigeon said the monkey was telling the truth.
  • The elephant said the monkey was lying.
  • The elephant served the monkey for one month.
  • The lion asked the other animals.
  • The monkey complained to the lion about the elephant.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Notice: the lion was the elephant's friend, yet he still sided with the truth. That is impartiality.
  • Vision: This page has no picture, only the story. Listen to it and pick up each animal's toy or card when its name comes: elephant, lion, bear, pigeon, monkey.
  • Understanding: Remember the story in three parts: the elephant lied, then the bear and the pigeon told the truth, then the lion was fair.
  • For teachers: Have the class act out the story: share out the roles of lion, fox, monkey, elephant, bear and pigeon; give children who cannot speak a masked, gesture-based role. In the storytelling activity a child may tell a story from home in their mother tongue, with pictures, or together with a buddy.
