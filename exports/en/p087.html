Exercise: Respecting others

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 87 of 96 (book page 83).

Activity

Have you been to another city, province or country? If yes, tell your friends about it: how the people's dress, way of living, food and drink, and daily routines are similar to yours and how they are different.

  • 1. Name the city, province or country you visited. If you have not been anywhere, tell us about a friend or neighbour who has come from another place.
  • 2. Talk about these four things: dress, way of living, food and drink, daily routines.
  • 3. Say: which thing is similar to yours?
  • 4. Say: which thing is different from yours?

Exercise

Question 1

Is this a right thing to do or a wrong thing?

  • 1. Pushing while standing in line (True / False)
  • 2. Caring for others (True / False)
  • 3. Honouring everyone and treating them with respect (True / False)
  • 4. Making fun of everyone (True / False)

Question 2

Write any two occasions when you stood in a queue and waited for your turn. (i) (ii)

  • 1. Remember the pictures on the previous page: the shop, the class, the road.
  • 2. Write two occasions when you yourself waited in a queue: (i) and (ii).

Question 3

How do you respect others? Give a few examples. (i) (ii) (iii)

  • 1. Think: how do you respect elders at home, and your teacher and classmates at school?
  • 2. Write three examples: (i), (ii) and (iii).

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: The answers are in your own life. Remember: where did you wait yesterday? Whom did you greet? Whom did you thank?
  • Movement: If writing on the lines is hard, record your answer by speaking, ask a partner to write it, or show it in a drawing. Use the big buttons for ✓ and ✗.
  • Understanding: In the activity compare just one thing, for example food: “What do people eat there? What do I eat?” Use pictures.
  • For teachers: Not every child has travelled — let them tell about a relative, a neighbour, or a place seen in a story or on TV, so no child is left out. Model turn-taking during the talk yourself: use a talking stick or ball, and praise answers given by signs or pictures equally.
