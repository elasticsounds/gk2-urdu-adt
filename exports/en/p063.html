Meat and leather from livestock; clothes from cotton

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 63 of 96 (book page 59).

Picture: A butcher is cutting meat at his shop, and meat is hanging behind him.

Caption: The meat of livestock is eaten.

Picture: Animal skins are hanging in rows in a factory.

Caption: The skin of livestock is used to make leather.

Activity

Complete the process given below by pasting pictures. Cotton, then thread, then cloth, then clothes.

  • 1. Look at the picture of cotton in the first circle.
  • 2. In the second circle, draw thread or stick on real thread.
  • 3. In the third circle, draw cloth or stick on a piece of cloth.
  • 4. In the fourth circle, draw clothes, such as a shirt.
  • 5. Trace the arrows with your finger and say the whole process aloud.

Picture: An open, soft, white cotton boll is growing on a cotton plant.

Caption: Cotton

Try it

Put the steps from cotton to clothes in the right order.

Put these in the right order:

  • Cloth
  • Clothes
  • Cotton
  • Thread

For teachers: Complete the activity by using drawings, pictures or real materials.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Follow the arrows: cotton, then thread, then cloth, then clothes. Look at your shirt and think: it was once cotton in a field too.
  • Vision: Instead of circles, use four boxes or plates. Put real cotton wool, thread, a piece of cloth and a small shirt or handkerchief in them, and touch them in order.
  • Movement: If drawing or pasting is hard, tell a buddy which thing goes in which circle, or slide the objects into place by hand.
  • For teachers: The pictures of meat and skins may feel strange to some children; explain gently that shoes, bags and belts are made of leather. In the activity, accept drawings, real objects or spoken answers.

Answers

Activity

  • Sample answer: Thread is made from cotton, cloth is made from thread, and clothes are made from cloth.
