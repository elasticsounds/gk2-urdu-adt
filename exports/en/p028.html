Christmas, Holi and Baisakhi

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 28 of 96 (book page 24).

Christmas

Every year on 25 December, Christians celebrate Christmas to mark the birthday of Hazrat Isa (peace be upon him). On this day they decorate Christmas trees and their homes, and give gifts to one another.

Picture: In a decorated room, children are decorating a green Christmas tree with coloured balls; there is a star on top of the tree and gifts tied with ribbons lie under it.

Holi

Holi is an important festival of Hindus. On this day Hindus come together, throw colours on one another and show their happiness.

Picture: In a green garden, children and grown-ups laugh and run as they throw colours on one another; their clothes and faces are covered in colours, and bowls of colour stand on a table.

Baisakhi

Baisakhi is a very old festival of the Sikh religion. Baisakhi is the first day of the Sikh new year. On this day Sikhs celebrate.

Picture: In a golden wheat field, Sikh men in colourful turbans dance the bhangra to the beat of a drum, and a woman carries a bundle of wheat on her head.

Do you know? Guru Nanak, the founder of the Sikh religion, was born in Nankana Sahib, Punjab.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Remember one sign for each festival: Christmas — a decorated tree; Holi — colours; Baisakhi — a wheat field and a drum. Look at the picture and name the festival.
  • Vision: Get to know each festival by touch or by sound: a small bell or a box with a ribbon for Christmas, a pinch of dry colour or flour for Holi, and an ear of wheat and the beat of a drum for Baisakhi.
  • Understanding: Read only one festival at a time. Look at the picture first, then say one sentence: “Christians celebrate Christmas.” Then move to the next festival.
  • For teachers: Give all three festivals equal time and respect. If there are Christian, Hindu or Sikh children in the class, invite them (if they wish) to talk about their festival. Do not force any child.
