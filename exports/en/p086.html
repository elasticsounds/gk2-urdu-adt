Good habits: respect and waiting for your turn

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 86 of 96 (book page 82).

Respecting one another and helping others are good habits.

Picture: In a park a boy is kindly taking an elderly man in a wheelchair for a walk. The elderly man is smiling.

Caption: Serving elders

Picture: In front of a counter, children and adults stand in a queue one behind the other, waiting for their turn.

Picture: On the road the vehicles have stopped and a boy holding his little sister's hand crosses at the zebra crossing. A man is crossing too.

Caption: Waiting for your turn on the road

Try it

Which habit is good and which is not? Put each in the right box.

Groups: Good habit; Not a good habit.

Items:

  • Crossing at the zebra crossing when vehicles have stopped
  • Jumping the queue to go to the front
  • Making a queue and waiting for your turn
  • Raising your hand before speaking in class
  • Running across the road between moving vehicles
  • Serving elders
  • Speaking while someone else is still talking

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: In each picture, find who is waiting. Then think about where you wait for your turn — at the tap, at the canteen, at the swings.
  • Vision: Listen to the descriptions of all four pictures. To understand a queue, stand one behind another with classmates and put your hand on the shoulder of the one in front.
  • Understanding: Play “First you, then me”: pass a ball in turns. Only the one holding the ball speaks.
  • For teachers: Practise with a real queue in class (for drinking water or handing in notebooks). For children who cannot stand long or who use a wheelchair, keep their place and turn in the queue. With the wheelchair picture, explain that asking permission before helping is respect.
