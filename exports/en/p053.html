Chapter 8: Animals

Learning outcomes

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

  • Name the animals around you, on land and in water.
  • Recognise that animals that live in water and animals that live on land have different features.
  • Recognise that all animals have babies, which grow up and become big.
  • Recognise different animals and their babies.
  • Recognise that the babies of some animals do not look like them when they are young.
  • Make a list of animals that give food to their babies and look after them.
  • Name the places where different animals live.

Animals live on our Earth along with people. Some of them live on land and some live in water.

On land, some animals walk and some crawl, while others fly in the air.

  • b) It crawls

2. How does a markhor move?

  • b) It crawls
  • c) It flies

3. What does a chukar partridge do in the air?

  • a) It crawls
  • b) It swims
  • c) It flies

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: First look at the three pictures at the bottom: the chukar partridge, the markhor and the snake. Think: which one flies? Which one walks? Which one crawls?
  • Hearing: Use your hands to show it: flap your arms for flying, walk two fingers for walking, and wiggle your hand for crawling.
  • Vision: The chukar partridge is a bird. The markhor is a mountain goat with big horns, and it is Pakistan's national animal. The snake is a long animal with no legs. Ask someone to let you hear their sounds or hold toy models of them.
  • For teachers: Ask the children to name an animal from their home or street, show it with a gesture, or make its sound. Accept every kind of answer.
