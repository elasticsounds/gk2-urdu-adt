Natural sources of water

  • 1. Rain
  • 2. Snow
  • 3. Underground water

The most important and biggest source of water is rain. Rainwater flows into ponds, lakes, canals and rivers.

Snow on glaciers and mountains melts and flows as water into streams, canals, ponds and lakes, and this water then travels along rivers into the sea.

As well as this, there is also a store of water deep under the ground.

Try it

See how well you remember the sources of water.

1. The most important and biggest source of water is ___.

  • a) the sea
  • b) rain
  • c) a waterfall

2. On glaciers and mountains, ___ melts and flows into the streams.

  • a) snow
  • b) sand
  • c) soil

3. This water travels along rivers into the ___.

  • a) pond
  • b) well
  • c) sea

4. There is also a store of water in the ___ of the ground.

  • a) depths
  • b) roof
  • c) air

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Remember the three natural sources on your fingers: rain from above, snow on the mountain, and water under the ground. Say them again with your hand held up high, then in the middle, then down low.
  • Vision: Put a piece of ice on your hand and feel it melt into water. Mountain snow melts in the same way and becomes rivers.
  • Hearing: Link a gesture to each picture: for rain, move your fingers downwards; for river, wave your hand; for snow, shiver. Write the new words 'glacier' and 'waterfall' on the board next to their pictures.
  • For teachers: Make a slope of sand with ice on top and show children how the melted water runs down as a 'river' into a basin that is the 'sea'. For the ordering task give sentence cards so children can answer by placing cards instead of writing.
