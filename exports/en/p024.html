Exercise: Rights and duties

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 24 of 96 (book page 20).

Exercise

Question 1

Some facilities are listed below. Put a tick (✓) on each facility that is in your area. Hospital ____ Airport ____ Police station ____ School ____ Park ____ Clean water supply ____ Bus stand ____ Post office ____

  • 1. Read or listen to these eight facilities one by one: hospital, airport, police station, school, park, clean water supply, bus stand, post office.
  • 2. For each one, think: "Is this in my area?" If you do not remember, ask your family.
  • 3. Put a tick (✓) on each facility that is there.
  • 4. Count how many ticks (✓) you made.

Question 2

Write two important public problems in your area. (i) ____ (ii) ____

  • 1. A "public problem" is a problem that many people have.
  • 2. Think about your area or ask your family.
  • 3. Write two problems.

Question 3

Write any two rights and two duties of citizens. (i) ____ (ii) ____ (i) ____ (ii) ____

  • 1. Read the "Rights and duties" table on page 19 again.
  • 2. Write two rights from the "It is my right" column.
  • 3. Write two duties from the "It is my duty" column.

Question 4

Who protects people's lives and property?

1. Which department protects people's lives and property?

  • a) Education department
  • b) Health department
  • c) Police department

Activity

The teacher should hold an election in class to choose a monitor. The teacher should introduce the children to the ballot box, the vote and all the steps, and so choose the class monitor.

  • 1. Children who want to be monitor give their names. Each candidate says what they will do for the class.
  • 2. Each child gets one slip of paper (a vote). On it, write the name or draw the symbol of the candidate you choose. Do not show it to anyone.
  • 3. Take turns to put your slip in the ballot box.
  • 4. The teacher counts the votes in front of everyone.
  • 5. The child with the most votes becomes the monitor. Everyone congratulates the monitor.

For teachers: Tell the children how they should look after the facilities provided by the government.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Questions 1 and 2 are about your own area. On your way home, look out for the facilities you can see, or ask your family. For Question 3, look at the table on page 19.
  • Movement: If making a tick is hard, answer by saying "yes" or "no", by nodding or shaking your head, or by putting on a sticker or a thumbprint. When voting, a partner can help to put the slip in the box.
  • Vision: In the election, stick a raised symbol for each candidate (such as a button, a piece of thread or a paper star) on the slip, so that a child can vote by touch.
  • For teachers: In the election give every child an equal chance to vote and to stand as a candidate, including children with disabilities. Keep the vote secret and encourage those who lose too. Questions 1 and 2 have no single right answer; accept the children's own observations.

Answers

Question 1

  • Sample answer: Example (the answer will be different for every area): hospital ✓, police station ✓, school ✓, park ✓, clean water supply ✓, bus stand ✓, post office ✓. There is no airport in our area.

Question 2

  • Sample answer: (i) Heaps of rubbish in the streets. (ii) Not enough clean drinking water. (The answer may be different for every area.)

Question 3

  • Sample answer: Rights: (i) Education, protection and health facilities. (ii) A supply of electricity, gas and clean drinking water. Duties: (i) Obeying the laws. (ii) Not damaging public or private property. (Any right or duty from the table is a correct answer.)

Question 4
