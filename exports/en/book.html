Our homeland Pakistan

Our homeland is Pakistan. It is in the continent of Asia. Pakistan came into being on 14 August 1947. Its capital is Islamabad.

Sindh, Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Punjab are the four provinces of Pakistan. As well as these, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir are areas administered by Pakistan. The capital of Azad Jammu and Kashmir is Muzaffarabad, while the capital of Gilgit-Baltistan is Gilgit.

By area, the biggest province is Balochistan, while by population the big province is Punjab.

Picture: A coloured map of Pakistan showing its provinces and areas. On the left is the biggest province, Balochistan. Sindh is at the bottom right, Punjab is in the middle, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is above, and Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir are at the very top. The neighbouring countries are Iran, Afghanistan, China and India, and the Arabian Sea is at the bottom. Labels name the parts of a map: title, scale, directions, boundaries and key.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: To remember the four provinces, count on four fingers: Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan. Then find each province's colour on the map.
  • Vision: Listen to the map description. Ask an adult to make a raised map of Pakistan on card with string or glue so you can feel the shapes of the provinces with your fingers.
  • Understanding: Learn only one thing at a time. First: 'Our country is Pakistan.' Then: 'The capital is Islamabad.' Then the names of the provinces.
  • For teachers: Let children find their own province and city on the map. They can point, say the name or sign. Link '14 August' with their memories of Independence Day (flags, the anthem).

The flag of Pakistan

Like every country's flag, the flag of Pakistan is its identity. It has the colours green and white.

In the flag, the green colour stands for Muslims and the white colour stands for people of other religions. The crescent moon on it is a symbol of progress, and the star is a symbol of light and knowledge.

Picture: The flag of Pakistan: there is a white stripe on the left, and the bigger part is green with a white crescent moon and a white five-pointed star in the middle.

Do you know? A large piece or area of dry land on Earth is called a continent. There are seven continents in the world.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: A way to remember the flag: the big part is green, the small stripe is white, and there is a moon and a star in the middle. Think: where have you seen the flag flying? For example, at school or on 14 August.
  • Vision: Listen to the description of the flag. Hold a cloth or paper flag and feel it; an adult can put glue or thread on the moon and star to make them raised.
  • Movement: You do not have to write in the map activity. You can say the names aloud or touch the right name on the screen.
  • For teachers: Point to the parts of the map one by one and have the whole class say the names. When you explain that green stands for Muslims and white stands for people of other religions, tell the children that the flag belongs to all Pakistanis.

The provinces of Pakistan

The four provinces of Pakistan and the federally administered areas each show their own way of life.

Sindh province

The people of Sindh speak the Sindhi language. For dress, they wear shalwar qameez, the ajrak and the Sindhi cap. For food, the people here like to eat rice, koki and spinach. Karachi is the capital of Sindh.

Do you know? Mohenjo-daro is in the Larkana district of Sindh.

Picture: A man wears a Sindhi cap on his head and a red printed ajrak on his shoulders; he is playing a stringed instrument.

Caption: Sindhi cap and ajrak

Picture: The ruins of the old city of Mohenjo-daro: broken brick walls with a high round mound behind them.

Caption: Mohenjo-daro

Note: Koki is a Sindhi bread made from flour, onion, coriander, green chilli, pomegranate seeds and cumin.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Look for three things about each province: language, dress and food. For Sindh, the language is Sindhi, the dress is the ajrak and the Sindhi cap, and the food is rice and koki.

  • Vision: To make the flag, feel a moon and a star cut out of card and stick them on green cloth or paper. Listen to the picture descriptions: an ajrak is a shawl with a red printed pattern.
  • For teachers: If you can, bring a real ajrak or Sindhi cap to class so the children can touch it and try it on. In the flag activity, accept every way of answering: drawing, sticking or saying it aloud.

Punjab province

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 9 of 96 (book page 5).

Punjab province

Punjab is the biggest province by population. Most people here speak the Punjabi language. Saraiki and Pothohari are also spoken.

Picture: The Badshahi Mosque in Lahore: a large red-brick building with three white domes and tall minarets at the corners, with a wide courtyard in front.

Caption: Badshahi Mosque, Lahore

The traditional dress of men is shalwar qameez, a turban, a kurta and a tehmad, while women wear shalwar qameez.

Lahore is the capital of Punjab. The people here like to eat mustard saag and butter, and to drink lassi.

Do you know? Kabaddi, the famous game of Punjab, is famous all over the world.

Picture: A kabaddi match: on a dusty field, five players in shorts are trying to catch one another, and many people are watching in front of the trees behind them.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province

The capital of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is Peshawar. This province is made up of beautiful mountains and valleys.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Remember three things about Punjab: the language is Punjabi, the dress is shalwar qameez and a turban, and the food is saag, butter and lassi. Have you ever drunk lassi or watched kabaddi?
  • Hearing: Learn the new words (turban, tehmad, lassi, kabaddi) with a picture or a gesture: wrapping a cloth round your head means turban, and drinking from a glass means lassi.
  • Vision: Listen to the picture descriptions. The Badshahi Mosque has three domes; put three upside-down bowls in a row to feel its shape.
  • For teachers: To explain the word 'population', have the children stand in two groups: the group with more children has the bigger population. Make sure children who use wheelchairs are counted too.

Do you know? Malam Jabba and Lake Saif-ul-Malook are very important places for tourism.

Picture: Bab-e-Khyber: a big gateway built of stone, with a round tower on each side and an arch over the road in the middle. The flag of Pakistan is flying on top.

Caption: Bab-e-Khyber

The famous foods here are pulao and chapli kabab. People also love to drink green tea.

Picture: Two round, flat chapli kababs on a plate, with slices of tomato on top and a piece of lemon beside them.

  • For everyone: Three things about Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: the language is Pashto, the dress is the Chitrali cap and Peshawari sandals, and the food is pulao and chapli kabab. Remember from the last page: its capital is Peshawar.
  • Vision: Listen to the picture descriptions. To understand the shape of Bab-e-Khyber, stand two glasses up and lay a book on top of them: two towers with a gate in between.
  • Hearing: Show the names of the languages (Pashto, Hindko, Khowar) written on cards. A video of the 'Attan' and 'Khattak' dances can be watched and understood even without sound.
  • For teachers: If a child in the class speaks Pashto or Hindko, ask them to teach everyone 'hello' or 'thank you' in their language. This builds respect for every child's language.

Balochistan province and Azad Jammu and Kashmir

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 11 of 96 (book page 7).

Balochistan province

By area, this is the biggest province of Pakistan. The Balochi, Pashto and Brahui languages are spoken in Balochistan. Men's dress is shalwar qameez and a turban, while women wear an embroidered kurta and shalwar. The special foods of Balochistan are sajji and khaddi kabab.

Quetta is the capital of Balochistan. The dry fruit from here is very famous.

Picture: The residence of the Quaid-e-Azam in Ziarat: a green, two-storey wooden building with steps in front and snow on the ground around it.

Caption: The residence of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah (may Allah have mercy on him) in Ziarat, a holiday spot

Do you know? "Balochi Chaap" and "Attan" are the famous dances of Balochistan.

Azad Jammu and Kashmir

Azad Jammu and Kashmir is an area administered by Pakistan. Muzaffarabad is the capital of Azad Jammu and Kashmir. The Pahari, Hindko, Gojri and Kashmiri languages are spoken here.

The favourite foods of the people of Kashmir are rice, Kashmiri kulcha and Kashmiri tea. Both men and women here wear the pheran.

Picture: A picture of the city of Muzaffarabad taken from above: roads, a bridge, white buildings and green mountains all around.

Caption: Muzaffarabad, Azad Jammu and Kashmir

Do you know? Azad Jammu and Kashmir has its own government, and it is an area administered by Pakistan.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: There are two places on this page. First read about Balochistan (its capital is Quetta and its food is sajji). Pause for a little while. Then read about Azad Jammu and Kashmir (its capital is Muzaffarabad and it has Kashmiri tea).
  • Understanding: To remember 'the biggest province', find the biggest coloured part on the map you saw earlier. That is Balochistan. Learn about only one place at a time.
  • Vision: Listen to the picture descriptions. Hold some dry fruit (almonds, raisins, walnuts) in your hand, then touch, smell and taste it. Balochistan is famous for it.
  • For teachers: Tell the children that the Quaid-e-Azam spent the last days of his life in Ziarat. Put one object on the table for each place (dry fruit for Balochistan, a cup of tea for Kashmir) so the children can name the place by looking at the object or touching it.

Gilgit-Baltistan

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 12 of 96 (book page 8).

Gilgit-Baltistan

Gilgit-Baltistan is in the north of Pakistan. Its capital is Gilgit. In Gilgit-Baltistan, people speak the Shina, Khowar, Burushaski and Balti languages.

The men here usually wear white shalwar qameez and the Gilgiti woollen cap. Women wear shalwar qameez and a cap. The people here eat food made from bread, butter and meat.

Picture: Two small children are standing in an apple orchard. The boy is wearing white shalwar qameez and a white woollen Gilgiti cap with a feather; the girl is wearing an embroidered cap and a white scarf.

Do you know? Rakaposhi is a famous mountain peak of Gilgit-Baltistan.

Activity

Collect pictures showing the culture of different areas of Pakistan, show them to your classmates and talk about them.

  • 1. With your family's help, collect pictures of the dress, foods and famous places of different areas from old newspapers, magazines or calendars.
  • 2. Write the name of the area beside each picture, or ask someone to write it for you.
  • 3. Show the pictures to your classmates.
  • 4. Say one thing about each picture: where is it from, and what is in it?

Activity

With your teachers' help, work in a group. Trace the provinces and Pakistan-administered areas given here on chart paper and make cutouts of them. Now join these cutouts together to complete the map of Pakistan. Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, Gilgit-Baltistan.

  • 1. Place thin paper over each coloured piece on the next page and trace its shape with a pencil.
  • 2. Draw the shape on chart paper and, with your teacher's help, cut it out with scissors.
  • 3. Write its name on each piece: Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, Gilgit-Baltistan.
  • 4. Join the pieces together like a puzzle. Look at the map on page 2 for help.
  • 5. Stick the finished map on a chart and put it up in the classroom.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: 'North' means the very top of the map. Gilgit-Baltistan is at the very top of Pakistan's map, where there are high snowy mountains.
  • Movement: If using scissors or tracing is hard for you, choose another job in the group: showing where the pieces go, saying the names or putting on the glue. Big pieces made of thick card are easier to hold.
  • Vision: Ask someone to make each piece from a different rough material (sandpaper, cloth, card) so that you can tell the provinces apart by touch and join the map together.
  • For teachers: Make the groups so that every child has a role: one traces, one cuts, one writes the names, one joins the pieces and one presents. In the picture-collecting activity, children who have no pictures can use the pictures in this book or draw their own.

The lion's justice

Now the fox asked the elephant to tell his story. The elephant looked towards the lion and said: "Your Majesty! This monkey is lying. I have not gone towards his area for many days. I had gone to visit the neighbouring jungle."

The elephant was sure that, since the lion was his friend, he would decide in his favour. The lion asked the other animals whether anyone had seen the elephant in the monkey's area.

The bear and the pigeon came forward and said: "Yes! The monkey is telling the truth. We saw the elephant troubling him." The lion looked at the elephant and said: "You not only troubled the monkey but also told a lie. Your punishment is to serve the monkey for one month and collect fruit for him."

All the animals were pleased with the lion's justice. The elephant was ashamed of his behaviour and admitted his mistake. The elephant then served the monkey for one month.

This story teaches us that we should always show justice and impartiality.
