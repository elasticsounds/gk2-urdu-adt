All people are equal

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 85 of 96 (book page 81).

Adnan, Arjeet and Joseph are friends. They belong to different religions. Their culture and way of living are different from one another. They help one another and respect what each other has to say.

Picture: Three friends stand side by side, smiling: one wears an orange head covering called a patka, one wears a black shalwar kameez and one wears a yellow kurta. There is a rainbow behind them.

All human beings are equal by birth and deserve honour and respect, and in the same way their rights are also equal and important.

Picture: In a classroom a teacher and children sit at desks, writing, and each in turn says something about respect. What they say is written below.

  • People feel happy when they are given respect.
  • It is important to respect the places of worship of all religions.
  • We all have equal rights.
  • To receive respect from others, we should give them respect.
  • Accept each other's religious and cultural differences and give one another honour and respect.
  • It is important to respect women.

Try it

Read the story of Adnan, Arjeet and Joseph and what the children in the class say. Then decide: true or false?

  • 1. All three friends belong to the same religion. (True / False)
  • 2. They respect what each other has to say. (True / False)
  • 3. All human beings are equal by birth. (True / False)
  • 4. People get upset when they are given respect. (True / False)
  • 5. It is important to respect the places of worship of all religions. (True / False)

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Look at the three friends in the first picture: their clothes are different but all three are smiling. In the second picture read each speech bubble one at a time.
  • Vision: In the classroom picture six sayings are written in speech bubbles. They are all in the list below the picture too — listen to them.
  • Understanding: Remember one sentence: “We are different, but equal.” Show two pencils of different colours — different colour, same job.
  • For teachers: When talking about religion and culture, do not single out any child or question a child about their religion in front of everyone. Ask how we are all alike (we all play, we all need love) — and include differences of disability with the same respect.

Answers

Try it

  • 1. False
  • 2. True
  • 3. True
  • 4. False
  • 5. True
