Activity

Look carefully at the pictures given below and tell your class how air is being used in these pictures.

  • 1. Look at the first picture (the windmill): what is turning its blades?
  • 2. Look at the second picture (the parachute): why is its top puffed up?
  • 3. Tell your class by speaking, by using signs or by drawing a picture.

The sun

The sun is the biggest source of light and heat. The sun is very important for the growth of plants and crops. Without it, crops cannot grow on the Earth.

Picture: A yellow sunflower is blooming in the bright sunshine.

Caption: For the growth of plants

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: We cannot see air, but we can feel it. Blow on your hand or wave a paper fan. This same air turns the windmill and holds up the parachute.
  • Vision: Make a paper pinwheel, blow on it and feel the spinning blades with your finger. Tie threads to the four corners of a handkerchief to make a small parachute, and feel it as it falls.
  • Understanding: Look at one picture at a time and ask just one question: “Where is the air?” The child can answer by putting a finger on the picture.
  • For teachers: For “tell your class”, accept signs, acting (turning the arms like a windmill) or a picture as well as speaking. An experiment with two plants, one kept in sunshine and one in shade, shows how important the sun is.

Answers

Activity

  • Sample answer: In a windmill, the wind turns the blades and this makes electricity. A parachute fills with air, so the man comes down to the ground slowly and safely.
