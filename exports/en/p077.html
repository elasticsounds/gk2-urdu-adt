Activity

Describe a situation when you helped others or others helped you.

  • 1. Remember a day when you helped someone, or someone helped you.
  • 2. Say: who was it? What happened?
  • 3. Say: how was the help given?
  • 4. Say: how did you both feel after the help?

Sharing things

Sharing things makes friendship grow. We should share our toys, books, pencils and so on with our friends and our brothers and sisters.

At home we should use our room, toys, television, furniture and so on together with our brothers and sisters and others.

Activity

Name any three things that you share with your friends or family.

  • 1. Think: what do you share with friends at school?
  • 2. Think: what do you share with family at home?
  • 3. Say the names of any three things.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: In all three pictures, find: who is helping and who is being helped?
  • Hearing: Learn the signs for “help” and “thank you”, and share your answer in the activity by signing, drawing or writing.
  • Movement: Instead of writing the names of three things, you can say them aloud or point to those things in the classroom.
  • For teachers: Put real objects (toy, book, pencil) in a basket and let pairs take turns sharing them. Give every child both roles — helper and helped — so that children with disabilities are not always only the ones “being helped”.
