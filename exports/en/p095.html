Exercise: impartiality

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 95 of 96 (book page 91).

Exercise

Question 1

Read or listen to each sentence. If it is right, press “True”. If it is not right, press “False”.

  • 1. Treating others well is a good habit. (True / False)
  • 2. Taking more than your share is a bad thing. (True / False)
  • 3. A person who does not admit their mistake is a great person. (True / False)
  • 4. We should choose to be fair in life. (True / False)

Question 2

Question 2. Did the bear and the pigeon do well by telling the lion the truth? What is your opinion?

  • 1. First write: was it good or not?
  • 2. Then write: why?

Question 3

Question 3. If you were in the lion's place, what decision would you give about the elephant?

  • 1. Remember: which two wrong things did the elephant do?
  • 2. Write what decision you would give.

Question 4

Question 4. Say aloud what you have learned from the story.

  • 1. Think: what good thing did the lion do?
  • 2. Say the lesson in one or two sentences.

For teachers: Tell the children about the results of being fair.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Before answering, recall the story: the monkey complained, the elephant lied, the bear and the pigeon told the truth, the lion was fair, the elephant admitted his mistake.
  • Movement: In Question 1, instead of writing ✓ or ✗ you can show thumbs up or thumbs down. For Questions 2 and 3, say your answers and let someone write them.
  • Hearing: Question 4 is oral; you may also answer in sign language, by drawing or in writing.
  • For teachers: Questions 2, 3 and 4 are opinion questions; accept every thoughtful, fair answer. In Question 3 remind children that a good decision repairs harm rather than causing pain. Show the results of fairness (trust, friendship, peace) with pictures or a short role play.

Answers

Question 1

  • 1. True
  • 2. True
  • 3. False
  • 4. True

Question 2

  • Sample answer: Yes, the bear and the pigeon did well by telling the truth. Because they told the truth, the lion could decide correctly and the monkey got justice.

Question 3

  • Sample answer: If I were in the lion's place, I would tell the elephant to say sorry to the monkey, give back his fruit and never trouble anyone again.

Question 4

  • Sample answer: From this story I learned that we should always show justice and impartiality.
