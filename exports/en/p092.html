Chapter 16: Impartiality: being fair

Picture: A judge's wooden gavel resting on a table with thick books behind it. A judge taps this gavel when giving a decision. The scales of justice are also shown faintly on the page.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

  • Recognise impartiality, which means being fair, and partiality, which means taking sides, in your daily life.
  • Recognise how a one-sided, unfair situation can be changed into a fair one.
  • If you have been unfair to others, accept responsibility for your behaviour and change it.

Try it

Impartiality means being equally fair to everyone. Partiality means siding only with your favourite. Say which action is fair.

Groups: Impartiality (fair); Partiality (unfair).

Items:

  • Blaming one person without listening to both
  • Letting all children take turns to play
  • Listening to both sides before deciding
  • Sharing sweets equally among everyone
  • Taking more than your share
  • Taking only your own friend into the team

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Remember the scales: when both pans are level, that is fairness. Impartiality means not leaning to one side.
  • Vision: Hold both hands out like a pair of scales. Put the same object in each hand (level means fair), then more in one hand (tilted = taking sides).
  • Understanding: Swap the hard words for easy ones: impartiality means fairness, the same for everyone. Partiality means unfairness.
  • For teachers: Show it with real objects: share six sweets between two children first as 5 and 1, then as 3 and 3, and ask which way is fair. Children answer with thumbs up or down, by speaking or by showing a card. If possible bring real scales to class so children can touch and explore them.
