Underground water: well, hand pump and tube well

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 41 of 96 (book page 37).

We get underground water from wells, hand pumps and tube wells.

Picture: A round well is built of stones, and a wooden bucket hangs on a rope from a wooden beam above it.

Caption: Well

Picture: Water is gushing from the thick pipe of a tube well into a tank.

Caption: Tube well

Picture: A green iron hand pump with a long handle stands in a grassy field, and a wooden bucket sits in front of it.

Caption: Hand pump

Activity

With your teacher's help, make a poster in a group that shows the importance of using water carefully.

  • 1. Sit with your group and think: how can we save water?
  • 2. At the top of the chart write in big letters: "Use water carefully".
  • 3. Draw or stick on two or three pictures, such as a tap turned off, a bucket, or a plant being watered.
  • 4. Colour it in and put the poster up in class.
  • 5. Tell the class what your poster says.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: One thing is the same in all three pictures: water is coming up from under the ground. From a well by pulling up a bucket, from a hand pump by working the handle, and from a tube well by running a motor.
  • Vision: If there is a hand pump nearby, hold its handle, move it up and down and listen for the water coming. Stick raised things onto the poster, such as cotton-wool drops and a tap made of string.
  • Movement: Each child can have a different job on the poster: one gives ideas, one chooses pictures, one picks the colours, and one sticks things on. Use thick markers and large pictures.
  • For teachers: Make mixed groups and give each child a clear role so that children with disabilities are not just onlookers. Put pictures or symbols beside the words on the poster so that children who cannot read also understand the message.

Answers

Activity

  • Sample answer: Poster message: "Water is life. Keep the tap turned off. Do not waste water." Beside it is a picture of a tap turned off and a drop of water.
