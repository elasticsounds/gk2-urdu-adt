Chapter 15: Forgiveness and forbearance

Picture: A large butterfly with white and black wings marked with fine lines.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

  • Know that our own words and actions can hurt others, and other people's words and actions can hurt us (such as telling lies, pushing others, using mocking words, etc.).
  • Understand that mistakes can happen while learning; it is not right to feel ashamed of this or to make fun of anyone.
  • Know that making fun of others can cause mental stress and pain.
  • Identify the things that can take away other people's hurt (such as saying sorry, admitting a mistake, doing something good for them).
  • Recognise that when people say sorry for a mistake, we should forgive them.

Try it

Look at each action. Does it hurt someone's heart or make it happy? Put it in the right box.

Groups: Actions that hurt; Actions that take the hurt away.

Items:

  • Admitting a mistake
  • Doing something good for them
  • Making fun of someone
  • Pushing others
  • Saying sorry
  • Telling lies

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: “Forgiveness and forbearance” means forgiving someone and letting the matter go. Remember: when did you last say “sorry” to someone?
  • Hearing: Agree on one sign for "sorry" and "forgive" (for example, a hand on the heart) and use the same sign throughout the chapter.
  • Understanding: Do not read all five points at once. Remember just two things: do not hurt anyone, and forgive.
  • For teachers: Explain the hard words (words and actions, mocking, admitting, forbearance) with simple words and facial expressions. Show happy and sad face cards and ask which action goes with which face; children may answer by speaking, pointing or holding up a card.

Answers

Try it
