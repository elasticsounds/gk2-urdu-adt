The story of Sara and Nida

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 89 of 96 (book page 85).

Today was Sara's first day at school. She was worried. The teacher seated her with Nida. Nida thought of some mischief. She kept calling her bad names. Sara felt very hurt. Tears came to her eyes. She sat quietly. All day she could not put her heart into her studies. Seeing her helplessness, Nida kept laughing at her. At home time Sara quietly got up and left.

The next day, when both came into the classroom, there was mischief on Nida's face again. Sara greeted her and sat down beside her. Today there was an Urdu test. Nida opened her bag but could not find a pencil in it. She became worried. Seeing her worry, Sara held out her own pencil to her.

Picture: Two girls sit at a round table with open books in a classroom. The girl on the left is laughing hard with her eyes shut, and the girl on the right is looking at her. Behind them are a bookshelf, a globe and a plant pot.

Nida felt ashamed. She realised that she had treated Sara badly, yet Sara had still helped her. Nida said sorry to Sara. Sara forgave her. Sara told her, "My mother says that forgiving is a good thing." Nida held out her hand in friendship to Sara. Sara said: "From today we are good friends."

Activity

Children tell everyone about a time in their own life when they forgave someone or said sorry to someone.

  • 1. Think: what happened? Who was it with?
  • 2. Say: who said sorry and who forgave?
  • 3. Say: how did you feel afterwards?
  • 4. Listen carefully to others and do not laugh at anyone.

Try it

Remember the story and play the game.

Put these in the right order:

  • Nida could not find a pencil in her bag.
  • Nida kept calling Sara bad names.
  • Nida said sorry to Sara.
  • Sara forgave her and they became friends.
  • Sara gave Nida her pencil.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: The story has two days. On day one Nida teased Sara. On day two Sara helped Nida. Think about the difference between the two days.
  • Vision: In the picture the laughing girl on the left is Nida and Sara is on the right. Hold a pencil while listening, and when Sara gives her pencil, hold yours out to your partner.
  • Hearing: Act the story out: a sad face, giving a pencil, hand on heart for "sorry", and shaking hands at the end.
  • For teachers: In the activity do not force any child to speak in front of everyone. A child may tell a partner quietly, draw a picture or show it with gestures. Share your own example first, and agree as a class that nobody laughs at anyone's story.

Answers

Activity
