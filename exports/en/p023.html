My rights and my duties

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 23 of 96 (book page 19).

It is the duty of the government to protect its citizens and to give every citizen the freedom to live according to their own religion. It should also respect their places of worship and their festivals.

Rights and duties

It is my right ______; It is my duty ______:

  • A good environment, a home, education, protection and health facilities.; Taking care of my health.
  • A supply of electricity, gas and clean drinking water.; Respecting and honouring older people and elders.
  • Freedom to choose an occupation.; Obeying the laws.
  • Shelters for people who have no one to support them.; Using gas, water and electricity carefully and not wasting them.
  • Not damaging public or private property.

Activity

Role play: Divide the children into groups. In each group, one child becomes a government officer and another plays the part of a citizen. Ask each group to do a role play, within the time given, that tells the duties of the government and the rights of citizens.

  • 1. Make a pair or a group. Decide who is the government officer and who is the citizen.
  • 2. The citizen asks for one of their rights, for example: "Sir, there is no clean water in our neighbourhood."
  • 3. The officer says what the government's duty is, for example: "Giving clean water is the government's duty. We will have the tap repaired."
  • 4. The officer reminds the citizen of the citizen's duty, for example: "Please do not waste water."
  • 5. Now swap roles and choose another right.

Do you know? We can become good citizens by obeying the laws.

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: A way to remember: a "right" is what I receive (bring one hand towards yourself). A "duty" is what I must do (stretch your hand forward). Every right comes with a duty.
  • Vision: The table has two columns. First listen to the four "It is my right" sentences, then the five "It is my duty" sentences. Stop after each sentence and say it again in your own words.
  • Understanding: In the role play, saying just one sentence is enough, such as "Water is my right." A child can also take part by holding up a picture card (tap, school, bulb).
  • For teachers: Every child should get a part in the role play: a child who cannot speak can "ask" for a right with gestures, picture cards or a written slip of paper; a child who uses a wheelchair can sit at the officer's desk. At the sentence about religious freedom, explain that we respect everyone's festivals and places of worship.

Answers

Activity

  • Sample answer: Citizen: "Education is my right. Please have a school built in our village." Officer: "Yes, building a school is the government's duty. Your duty is not to damage the things in the school."
