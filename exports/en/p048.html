Roots that we eat

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 48 of 96 (book page 44).

Activity

Students should collect different kinds of leaves, put them on chart paper, draw their outlines and colour them in.

  • 1. Collect 3 or 4 leaves of different shapes that have fallen on the ground. Do not pull leaves off a plant.
  • 2. Put one leaf on the chart and hold it down with one hand.
  • 3. Move the pencil all the way round the edge of the leaf. The outline is made!
  • 4. Colour in the outline and write the name of the shape below it: broad, oval, small, or long and narrow.

Think and say

Can you name some other roots that we eat?

  • 1. Think: which vegetables that are cooked in your home grow under the ground?
  • 2. Write or say one or two names other than the four roots given in the book (sweet potato, turnip, carrot, radish).

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: A way to remember: a root grows under the ground. Carrots, radishes, turnips and sweet potatoes are dug out of the soil.
  • Vision: Hold a real carrot, radish and turnip. Get to know them by feeling their shape and smelling them: the carrot and the radish are long, and the turnip is round.
  • Movement: If moving a pencil round the leaf is hard, put the leaf under the paper and rub a thick crayon over it, or let a partner hold the leaf still.
  • For teachers: Bring real carrots, radishes and turnips, with their leaves, to class so that children can see that the part we eat is below the leaves: it is the root. When collecting leaves, pair children who use a wheelchair or have low vision with a buddy, and let every child choose at least one leaf themselves.
