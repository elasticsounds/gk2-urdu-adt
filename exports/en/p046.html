The parts of a plant and what they do

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 46 of 96 (book page 42).

Plants are very important in our lives. They give us oxygen to breathe, and fruit and other things to eat.

Just as the human body has different parts, a plant has different parts too. Each part does its own job. These parts are the root, the stem, the leaves and the flower.

The parts of a plant and what they do

Picture: This is a picture of a sunflower plant. At the top is a yellow flower, in the middle are a green stem and green leaves, and at the bottom brown roots spread out under the ground.

I am the flower: I make the plant more beautiful. I make the plant's fruit and seeds.

I am the leaf: together with water, air and light, I make the plant's food.

I am the stem: I carry the water that comes from the root to the branches and leaves.

I am the root: I live under the ground. I hold the plant firmly in the soil. I soak up water from the soil for the plant and send it up to the stem.

Do you know? Plants need soil, water, air and sunlight to stay alive and grow.

Try it

Each part of the plant is telling us its job. Match each part to its job.

First list:

  • 2. Leaf

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Look at the picture from bottom to top: root, then stem, then leaf, then flower. This is the same path that water takes on its way up.
  • For everyone: Remember with your body: your feet are the root, your legs and back are the stem, your hands are the leaves and your face is the flower. Stand up, touch each part and say its name.
  • Vision: Hold a real plant with its roots. Feel and get to know the thread-like roots, the hard straight stem, the flat leaves and the soft flower.
  • For teachers: Give four children the roles of flower, leaf, stem and root; each child says their "I am the …" line or shows it with a gesture. A child who cannot speak can hold up the card for their part.
