Natural resources and their importance

General Knowledge, Class 2. Page 31 of 96 (book page 27).

Activity

Divide the children into groups and give each group one part of the school. Ask one group to make a list of the living things in the school and the other group a list of the non-living things, and then let them talk about their lists together.

  • 1. Go with your group to the part of the school your teacher tells you (for example the courtyard, the classroom or the garden).
  • 2. Look carefully. One group makes a list of living things and the other group makes a list of non-living things.
  • 3. Write the name of each thing or draw a picture of it.
  • 4. Come back, read your list to the other group and listen to their list.

Think about it!

A car does move, but is it a living thing? Think and then answer.

1. A car or a bus goes from one place to another. Is it a living thing?

  • a) Yes, it is living
  • b) No, it is non-living

2. What is the reason?

  • a) It does not move by itself, does not grow and does not have young ones
  • b) It has wheels
  • c) It goes very fast

Natural resources and their importance

The things that nature has given us are called natural resources. Allah Almighty has created natural resources, which meet the needs of human beings so that they can stay alive. There are several kinds of natural resources: land, water, sun and air resources.

From the sun we get light and heat, while water is very important for drinking and air for breathing.

Picture: Sunlight shines through the branches of a big green tree.

Picture: A reddish-brown cow is standing.

Picture: A yellow windmill floats on blue water, with a forest behind it.

Try it

What do we use each natural resource for? Match the pairs.

1. The things nature has given us are called ___.

  • a) toys
  • b) natural resources
  • c) festivals

2. From the sun we get light and ___.

  • a) heat
  • b) water
  • c) soil

3. ___ is very important for breathing.

  • a) Soil
  • b) Light
  • c) Air

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Count the four natural resources on your fingers: land, water, sun and air. For each one, think: how did I use it today? (I drank water, breathed air, played in the sunshine and walked on the ground.)
  • Vision: Feel the natural resources: the warmth of the sun on your face, air on your hand, water on your fingers and soil in your fist. In the school activity, work with a partner and get to know things by touching them.
  • Movement: A child who cannot walk around the school can look at things from their own place (through the window or in the room) and name them, while a partner writes them down. Ticking pictures instead of writing a list is fine too.
  • For teachers: Share out the jobs in each group: one child looks, one writes or draws, and one reads out the list, so that every child takes part in the way they are able. In the discussion, accept speaking, signs and pictures alike.
