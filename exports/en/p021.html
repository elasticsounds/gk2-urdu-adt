Chapter 3: Rights and duties

Learning outcomes

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

  • Say what government means.
  • Identify the facilities that the government provides for citizens.
  • List any three rights of citizens (food, free education, protection, health and equality).
  • Understand that every citizen has the right to live freely according to their own religion.
  • Identify your own responsibilities that go with the rights you have.

The government runs the system of the country. For the good of its citizens, it provides facilities for health, clean water, electricity, gas, playgrounds for fun and play, and good-quality education.

Try it

Read or listen to the paragraph above. Then answer: what does the government do for us?

1. The government runs the ___ of the country.

  • a) factory
  • b) system
  • c) field

2. For fun and play, the government provides ___.

  • a) playgrounds
  • b) toys
  • c) sweets

3. The government gives these facilities for the good of its ___.

  • a) enemies
  • b) animals
  • c) citizens

Helpful hints

  • For everyone: Think of "government" like this: just as Mum and Dad run the home and the head teacher runs the school, the government runs the whole country.
  • Vision: As you listen to the paragraph, count the facilities the government gives on your fingers: health, clean water, electricity, gas, playgrounds, education. That makes six in all.
  • Understanding: Look around you: water from the tap, light from the bulb, your school. These are all facilities. Touch or point to each one and say its name.
  • For teachers: Give a simple example of a "right" and a "duty": coming to school is my right; keeping the classroom clean is my duty. Agree on one gesture for each of the two words and use the same gestures throughout the chapter.

Answers

Try it

  • 1. b) system
  • 2. a) playgrounds
  • 3. c) citizens
